---

## Context

Today I decided to write about something that I find fascinating: how complicated behavior can emerge from very simple rules.

## Reflections

A system does not necessarily need a complicated central controller to produce complicated behavior.

A few simple components interacting with each other can produce patterns that none of the individual components contain by themselves.

Ant colonies are a classic example.

An individual ant follows relatively simple rules, but thousands of ants interacting can create surprisingly sophisticated behavior.

## A question

At what point does a collection of simple components become something we would call "intelligent"?

And is intelligence itself an emergent property?

## Takeaway

Sometimes understanding the rules of the individual parts is not enough.

We also need to understand what happens when the parts interact.
